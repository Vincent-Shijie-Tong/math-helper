# Project brief: Math Helper
## The competition
- I’m entering the Presidential SI Challenge (Standard Track), a national K–12 competition. My age group: Elementary (grades K–5)
- Judges score only a written entry (a PDF of up to 10 pages) and a video of up to 4 minutes. They reward a clear understanding of the problem and a sensible use of AI.
- My deadlines: The two deadline I can't miss are December 18th, 2026 and December 23, 2026. By December 18th, 2026 my teacher has submit the registration and by December 23, 2026 I have to submit my project.
## The problem
- People in my school struggle with homework, learning, and tests because no one helps them, guides them, or tutors them.
- Impact area: School and learning
- Why it matters (impact): If my project works then these people will score higher on their test, learn faster, and get better at everything.
- Evidence it’s real: Tutoring helped students in Chicago. In two studies of Chicago high school students, students who took part in a math tutoring program got higher math test scores and better math grades. University of Chicago Education Lab — Chicago tutoring study
- What people told me: Mr. Hat Guy told me he got all 4s after getting tutoring from people. The grading system is 1-4. 4 is the highest score. Mr. Glasses Guy told me he got better at grammar after he got tutoring from a grammar teacher.
## My solution
- My AI’s job: My AI helps by giving students tutoring and explaining problems, which works better than tutors because it is free and it is available 24/7.
- Tools that already exist, and how mine is different: Some tools are Khan Academy and Khanmingo. Mine is different since it is free and it is one on one so the website will explain what you did wrong and how to fix it.
- Safety plan: Physical safety — Dangerous advice. A student could follow unsafe instructions for a science experiment or ask how to hurt someone.	Block instructions that could cause harm. Offer a safe explanation for schoolwork. If someone could be hurt right now, tell the student to get a trusted adult immediately.
Emotional safety — Mean replies or bullying. The AI could shame a student for making a mistake or be asked to write a cruel message about a classmate.	Give the AI a rule to respond kindly, explain mistakes without blame, and refuse to write bullying messages. If a student says they’re being bullied, encourage them to tell a trusted adult.
Data security — Students share private details. They might type a full name, password, address, or student ID, or upload a worksheet with their name on it.	Put a reminder next to the chat box: “Share only the homework question. Remove names and other private details first.”
Data security — Someone else can see the chat. The company running the AI or someone managing the app may be able to read messages.	Turn off chat history for the first version. Check what the AI company still saves, then tell students who can see messages and how long they’re kept.
Wrong answers — The AI can sound sure and still be wrong. A student could learn the wrong rule or practice the wrong steps for a test.	Show the steps so students can check them. When the AI is unsure, have it say so and point students to their class notes, textbook, or teacher.
## My MVP features (build these first, in this order)
1. The user can ask the AI to suggest study tips for upcoming tests.
2. The user can ask the AI for help with homework questions anytime.
3. The user can receive step-by-step explanations for solving math problems.
4. The user can receive feedback on their answers to check for correctness.
5. The AI gives correct, safe answers to real questions.

## My AI’s instructions
- When it answers: The user types a problem or inserts a photo.
- Its job: To look at the problem in or not in a photo and then explain how to do the problem or what did they do wrong. It also gives the answer if they ask for one.
- Who it talks to and how to sound: My AI is talking to a human and it should sound helpful and comforting
- Facts it must use: Math formuals and how to do math.
- Rules it must follow: Answer something not math related or give a wrong answer or wrong advice
- How to answer: It should answer nicely and the answer should be 10-100 words long. What comes first is how to do the problem and then after that it should give them a problem to try and the answer to the problem the human put in.
